# Swap espresso bands for ivory and linden

Čebelarstvo Šemen's shop feels heavy because of how many dark bands it has and where they sit, not because brown is the wrong colour. The homepage stacks two full-width espresso (#2B2420) blocks in the middle of the page and adds a third as the footer, while both of the client's references keep dark to the frame: about 13% of the page in old Rast and about 7% in the Behance concept, whose footer brown (#30261C) is effectively Šemen's own. The recommended direction, "Lipa & slonovina" (linden and ivory), puts an ivory #FAF7F0 page under white surfaces and one pale linden band, gives calls to action to deep linden green #3E5234 (8.53:1 with white text), and keeps the brand gold #C99A5C for ornament only, because it reaches just 2.37:1 on ivory. Gold-looking text goes in bronze #8A6428 (4.98:1), and the whole dark budget is spent on a single #2E3A29 footer that absorbs the newsletter. Headings move to sentence-case Cormorant over Jost body text; both fonts are in Shopify's own library, cover č, š and ž, and load from Shopify's servers rather than Google's, so the German Google Fonts ruling never comes into play, and they replace a heading font Shopify has already deprecated. The homepage follows old Rast's rhythm, from a static light hero and the eight honeys in a fixed grid on identical plates to the beehouse visit on the one tinted band, with 96–128 px of air between sections and only short, gentle fades for motion. The biggest risk is not visual: Šemen's last public Slovenski med ZGO listing expired in October 2021, and two of its eight honeys fall outside the protected specification, so the protected-origin (PGI) mark may appear only beside verified, sealed jars.

## Both references confine dark to the frame; Šemen stacks it mid-page

### Old Rast is light; today's rast.ch is the heavy version

The client's Pinterest image of Rast Kaffee is not today's rast.ch but the roaster's earlier site, whose footer reads "©2013"; the domain switched to a different page in June 2020 ([Wayback CDX](https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=rast.ch&output=txt&fl=timestamp,statuscode,digest&collapse=timestamp:4)). Its elegance comes from discipline. The page runs on two almost identical light tones, #F6F5F3 for the page and a barely deeper #F3F0EB for a single map band. A single charcoal, #1E1E1C in the stylesheet of the time, appears only in the header bar, the one button and the footer ([archived app.css](https://web.archive.org/web/20180727101537/https://www.rast.ch/static/css/app.css?r=25)), and header and footer together take **about 13% of the page height** ([client screenshot](C:/Users/UPORAB~1/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--Users-Uporabnik-Desktop-Work-Stranke-2026-prodaja-medu-website/7c41fca9-10a2-48ee-a221-6b9cb5504eca/images/26.png)). Five identical white discs present the coffees as specimens, each with its origin in small spaced capitals above the name, and no prices, hearts or cart buttons. Three equal portrait tiles follow, then a deliberately long pause, then one information band: a world map of origins.

Today's rast.ch shows how the same brand can turn heavy. It moved its headlines to sentence-case serif type, but three black zones now cover **about one third of the desktop page** ([rast.ch](https://www.rast.ch/de)). That is exactly the heaviness the client dislikes, so he is reacting to the old site's lightness rather than to the brand. The live site still offers two things worth taking: a strict 128 px gap between sections, and the move from all-caps to sentence-case serif headings.

### The Behance concept: keep its warmth, not its template habits

Reference A is a 2023 Behance concept by Nirmal Kumar, not a working shop ([Behance](https://www.behance.net/gallery/177842783/Coffee-Shop-Landing-Page-Design)). What the client most likely responds to is its warmth. A beige canvas (#E2D9C8) covers about 62% of the pixels on the concept's mobile board, near-white panels hold soft rounded cards and warm food photography, and dark brown appears only in small buttons and in a footer that takes about 7% of the page ([desktop board](https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400_webp/ce6495177842783.64dda5174a2c9.png)). That footer brown is practically #2B2420, so the colour itself is clearly acceptable to him; what he dislikes is the dark blocks in the middle of his own homepage.

Much of the rest would date the shop the moment it was copied. That includes the content boxed on a coloured canvas, the Poppins-like geometric sans with a script label, and the cut-out coffee beans bleeding off the page edges. It also includes the generic icon row, an "Order Now" button and heart on every card, testimonial cards with avatars and stars, a "15% off" newsletter, and four near-identical beiges and greys that look muddy rather than intentional. Its blocks also sit only about 44 px apart, against 128 px on today's rast.ch, and that spacing alone explains much of why one looks like a template and the other like an editorial site.

The same budget holds across the light, elegant food sites the research team captured on 20 October 2026. Honey producers follow the same logic and rarely use brown for it. Mieli Thun keeps dark to its hero and a single bottle-green band and ends on a cream footer ([mielithun.it](https://www.mielithun.it/)). Comvita's only large dark-brown area is its footer, with contrast in the middle of the page coming from green ([comvita.com](https://comvita.com/)). Beechworth Honey spends its one deep-green block on "Visit Us" ([beechworthhoney.com.au](https://www.beechworthhoney.com.au/)), and Flamingo Estate uses olive instead of black throughout ([flamingoestate.com](https://flamingoestate.com/)).

That gives Šemen a working budget. The base colour covers at least 70% of the page height, the tint no more than 20% in at most two bands that never sit next to each other, and dark no more than 10–15% in a single closing block, with a dark hero photograph counting as a dark zone. The current homepage runs hero, products, quality, family story (espresso), numbers (espresso), production (cream), testimonials, blog, newsletter and an espresso footer. That exceeds the budget and breaks the project's own rule of "few dark #2b2420 sections, never several in a row". The espresso colour is typed directly into the code **22 times across 13 section, snippet and footer files**, plus seven more times in the local page-template settings, so part of the fix is a code clean-up, described in the build notes below.

## Ivory, linden green and gold-as-ornament pass every contrast check

### Gold works as decoration, not as text

Gold is the brand's most precious colour and its least readable one. The accessibility standard (WCAG 2.2, level AA) asks for a contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (about 24 px, or 18.5 px bold), and the computed ratio may not be rounded up, so 4.499:1 fails ([W3C Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). On white, ivory or cream, **every gold lighter than about #9A6824 fails 4.5:1**.

Šemen's two near-identical golds, #C3935B and #C99A5C, reach only 2.29–2.74:1 on light backgrounds. They pass only on espresso (5.55:1 and 6.00:1), which is one reason the current design keeps reaching for dark bands. #C99A5C even fails the 3:1 bar for large headings, and amber #B07A2E clears it only from 24 px up (3.46:1 on ivory). In Cormorant's thin strokes, amber also runs into the W3C warning that thin fonts look fainter than their nominal colour.

The fix is to give gold three separate jobs. **Ornament gold #C99A5C**, which replaces #C3935B everywhere, stays for the crest, fine rules, icons and dividers; text that is part of a logo has no contrast requirement, so the crest itself does not change. **Text gold #8A6428**, a bronze, carries small labels and passes on the page, tint and white backgrounds alike (4.61–5.33:1). **Honey fill #C99A5C** can serve as a secondary button, but only with dark text on it (6.16:1), because white text on it fails (2.54:1).

### Linden green replaces terracotta for calls to action

The terracotta button colour (#BB5237) should retire. White text on it passes (4.79:1), but as text it fails on both cream (4.33:1) and ivory (4.48:1). Its hue sits right next to amber honey, so it competes with the product, and beside espresso and gold it reads rustic rather than refined. The best counter-example is Mieli Thun, whose orange buttons (#E95A0C) look premium on an Italian honey site ([mielithun.it](https://www.mielithun.it/)), but Mieli Thun pairs its orange with white and bottle green, not with brown and gold.

**Deep linden green #3E5234** is the stronger replacement. It is the complementary colour to amber, so jars and honey photographs glow against it, and gold on green is a classic heraldic pairing for a gold crest. It suggests meadow, linden and the Mura floodplain rather than coffee or chocolate. It is also how the elegant honey producers above add depth without brown, and the closest Slovenian peer, Čebelarstvo Noč, a family business that also keeps AŽ hives and Carniolan bees, already pairs deep green #123D2E with cream and gold ([cebelarstvo-noc.si](https://cebelarstvo-noc.si/)).

Green also fits current colour trends without chasing them. Pantone's 2026 colour is Cloud Dancer, a soft white that replaced 2025's Mocha Mousse brown ([The Dieline](https://thedieline.com/pantones-2026-color-of-the-year-is-cloud-dancer-its-white); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/pantone-names-2026-color-year-rcna247366)). Wallpaper*'s 2026 forecast pairs deep teal (WGSN's Transformative Teal) and khaki with a continuing taste for chocolate brown ([Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/design-interiors/colour-trends-2026)). Brown therefore survives as the warm near-black text colour rather than as bands, and linden sits close to the teal trend while being less likely to date with it.

Three things about the light tones matter more than they look. First, the separate cream should retire: ivory and the current cream #F7F3EA differ by a lightness step of only 1.03, and two nearly identical beiges look accidental, as they do in Reference A. Second, the linden band works through hue rather than darkness. It is only 1.07 darker than ivory, and the green cast is what makes it read as a band. If the client prefers a honey-coloured band, acacia #F6EDD3 can be the one tint instead, since every text colour passes on it (text 13.41, text gold 4.56, green 7.30), but honey buttons and amber jars must stay off it because honey on honey disappears (1.80:1). Third, the wheat plate sits within the reference sites' range. Its step from ivory (1.10) falls inside the 1.06–1.14 I measured between page and product plate at La Cabra, Sightglass, Flamingo and Beechworth, and white jar labels stand out against it (1.18), where a white plate would swallow them. Bronze text on wheat only just passes (4.50), so small labels belong below the plate, not inside it.

### Two alternatives, and a brown fallback

Two complete alternatives were also checked, and every text pair in both passes AA. "Pergament & črnilo" (parchment and ink) uses parchment #F8F4EB, an oat band, ink-blue #2A3F5E buttons and a #1D2631 footer; it is the most formal, printed-heritage option, but cooler and less food-like. ## Cormorant and Jost from Shopify's library settle style, č š ž and GDPR together

### The heading font has to change anyway

The theme's local settings set headings in Zurich Extended (`zurich_extended_n4`) and body text in Poppins. Shopify lists Zurich Extended as deprecated, with Epilogue as its replacement ([Shopify fonts doc](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/settings/fonts.md)), and since August 2025 it has been swapping deprecated fonts automatically ([Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/font-library-updates-automatic-replacement-for-deprecated-fonts)). The live store may therefore already show Epilogue, which is worth checking in the theme editor because the local settings file may be out of date. Either way, the redesign is the moment to choose a heading font on purpose.

### Cormorant over Jost

The recommendation is **Cormorant** for headings, a Garamond-style display serif by Christian Thalmann with a calligraphic italic ([Cormorant on GitHub](https://github.com/CatharsisFonts/Cormorant)), over **Jost**, Owen Earl's clean, Futura-like sans. Both are free open-licence (OFL) fonts that contain Č č Š š Ž ž Ć ć Đ đ and the € sign ([Cormorant metadata](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/cormorant/METADATA.pb); [Jost metadata](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/jost/METADATA.pb)). Both are in Shopify's font library as `cormorant_n5` and `jost_n4`, and Shopify's files include the Latin Extended-A character range that holds Č, Š and Ž ([Shopify fonts doc](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/settings/fonts)). The theme reaches weight 600 and the italics through Liquid's `font_modify` filter, and the pair is light to load: about 41 KB and 15 KB per weight, for the Latin and extended-Latin character sets combined.

EB Garamond over Work Sans is the more bookish alternative and has true small capitals, but EB Garamond's extended-Latin file weighs 55.6 KB per weight. Fraunces over Figtree is the warmest pairing, but food brands have used Fraunces widely since 2020 and it has no small capitals. Italiana is out because it lacks č, š and ž altogether, Playfair Display and Montserrat are overused, and Bodoni Moda reads as fashion or cosmetics.

Poppins deserves a fair hearing, since Mariage Frères sets its body text in Poppins under engraved capitals ([mariagefreres.com](https://www.mariagefreres.com/en/)). But its tall lowercase letters (an x-height of 0.548 of the font size, against Jost's 0.46) still look app-like, and a Poppins-style sans with a script label is one of Reference A's template signatures. **Marcellus**, also in Shopify's library, can serve as an optional third font for crest-like capital lockups such as "OD LETA 1982", used sparingly.

### Fonts served by Shopify avoid the Google Fonts ruling

On 20 January 2022, the Munich Regional Court (LG München I, 3 O 17493/20) ruled that loading Google Fonts from Google's servers without consent unlawfully sent visitors' IP addresses to Google. It rejected the "legitimate interest" defence, because hosting the fonts locally would have done the same job without the transfer, and awarded €100 in damages plus an injunction ([Dr. Datenschutz](https://www.dr-datenschutz.de/schadensersatz-urteil-google-fonts-und-die-dsgvo/)). Later German and Austrian courts threw out the mass warning letters, generated by crawlers, as an abuse of rights ([Der Betrieb](https://der-betrieb.de/meldungen/google-fonts-abmahnwelle-war-rechtsmissbraeuchlich); [futurezone](https://futurezone.at/netzpolitik/google-fonts-abmahnungen-klage-gerichtsurteil-missbraeuchlich-berufungsgericht/402911253)), but none declared loading fonts from Google lawful. The EU–US Data Privacy Framework, upheld by the EU General Court on 3 September 2025 ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/eus-general-court-confirms-adequacy-of-eu-u-s-data-privacy-framework)), settles whether data may go to the US. It does not settle whether the transfer was necessary in the first place. A Munich ruling is persuasive in Slovenia rather than binding, and the research found no guidance on this from the Slovenian data protection authority (Informacijski pooblaščenec).

The practical answer is still simple. Fonts from the Shopify library load from Shopify's own servers, which already host the shop, so no extra company receives visitor data and fonts need no cookie-banner entry ([Shopify fonts doc](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/settings/fonts.md)). The Molding theme's two "Google font" text fields must stay empty permanently, because filling either one makes the theme load the font from fonts.googleapis.com (`snippets/header-css.liquid` lines 70, 100 and 125; `templates/gift_card.liquid` lines 48, 74 and 94). Font files belong in the theme's own assets only for features Shopify cannot provide, such as true small capitals. Then they should be built from the full google/fonts repository, because the files served by Google's API and by Fontsource lack small capitals and old-style figures, as the research team measured. The fonts themselves are chosen in the theme editor, since `config/settings_data.json` belongs to the editor under the project's rules.

### Typography rules

Each typographic device gets one job. Headings are serif and sentence case, never all caps, because Slovene words run long and all caps is what dates the old Rast. They use weight 500 from 40 px up and 600 between 22 and 32 px, where Cormorant's thin strokes would otherwise fade. Emphasis is italic, never bold, with one phrase per heading set in the italic, as in "Med *iz našega čebelnjaka*".

Uppercase is reserved for small labels, navigation and buttons, all in Jost 500, with letter spacing that shrinks as size grows: about 0.18em at 12 px, 0.14em at 13–14 px, and 0.08–0.10em from 16 px up. These values are common typographic practice rather than a published standard. Two-line labels need a line height of at least 1.3 so the háčeks on Č, Š and Ž clear the line above, and lowercase text never gets extra letter spacing. Small labels above headings appear on alternate sections at most; the others open with a 40 × 1 px gold rule or a small bee taken from the crest, which is what stops the page looking like a Shopify template.

Cormorant never goes below 22 px, because its lowercase letters are about 16% shorter than Jost's. Very thin weights, outline text and fake small capitals are all out, and with Shopify-served files the uppercase sans label replaces small capitals. Body text runs at 16–17 px, with a line height of 1.6–1.7 and 65–72 characters per line.

## A Rast-style rhythm rebuilds the homepage around eight honeys and the beehouse

### Header and hero: solid, light and static

The header should be solid white and sit above the hero rather than over it. It stays fixed while the page scrolls, gets slightly shorter as it does, and closes with a 1 px rule in the text colour at 8–12% opacity. A centred crest with the menu split either side gives the most heritage feel, as on Mieli Thun, and Tiptree also centres its logo ([mielithun.it](https://www.mielithun.it/); [tiptree.com](https://www.tiptree.com/)); a left-aligned crest is the conventional alternative. The menu runs in 13 px Jost capitals and includes a top-level "Obisk" item, the way Beechworth and Daylesford give visits their own menu entry ([beechworthhoney.com.au](https://www.beechworthhoney.com.au/); [daylesford.com](https://www.daylesford.com/)), and there is no coloured announcement bar. A header laid transparently over the photo would only work if every hero image were dark at the top, and Nielsen Norman Group's (NN/g) guidance on text over images asks for a solution that works for the worst-case image ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-over-images/)). The gold crest on white repeats the cream-and-gold jar label. If its fine lines fade at header size, a slightly deeper gold for the header version is a readability choice rather than a compliance one, because logos are exempt. On mobile the header is 56–64 px tall and every tap target at least 44 px.

The hero should be one still image, not a slideshow. NN/g notes that a static hero "may be a better choice", warns against advancing slides automatically on mobile, and calls dots "a particularly poor cue" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-carousels/)). The Baymard Institute found usability problems on **46% of sites with a homepage carousel** and says auto-rotation "should be avoided on mobile" ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/homepage-carousel)). If the client insists on slides, he should keep no more than three, manual only, paused on hover and stopped for good after any interaction.

The photograph should be light and warm, such as the painted beehouse in daylight, the Mura floodplain or a jar lit from behind, because a dark hero photo would count against the dark budget the footer already spends. The headline is one sentence-case Cormorant line at 48–72 px, bottom left, over a gradient that darkens only the lower part of the image (roughly 0 to 45% black across the bottom 40%), so white text stays readable on any photo; one main button and one text link sit beside it. Technically, the hero needs a separate 4:5 mobile crop through a `<picture>` element, and it loads first (`fetchpriority="high"`), never lazily and never with a fade-in, as Shopify's performance guidance requires ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/best-practices/performance)). The existing benefits card can keep overlapping the hero's bottom edge, reduced to three items separated by thin vertical rules and without coloured icons, much as Sightglass floats a card over its hero ([sightglasscoffee.com](https://sightglasscoffee.com)). On mobile it becomes a plain row under the hero.

| 8 | Footer with newsletter | Linden #2E3A29 | Newsletter at the top of the block, then link columns and single-colour marks | Separate newsletter band and espresso footer |

A fixed grid replaces the carousel because eight honeys fit exactly in four columns and two rows, need no JavaScript, and avoid the carousel problems above. Folding the numbers into the family section removes both dark blocks from the middle of the page in one move, and every figure has a source. The shop's own copy dates the family to 1982 (`sections/footer-group.json`). The national beekeeping association's (ČZS) catalogue of apitourism providers records "Čebelarimo s 700 družinami. Čebele prevažamo na različne paše po Sloveniji" (we keep 700 colonies and move them to pastures across Slovenia), as well as a beehouse with 40 hives adapted for breathing in the hive air ([ČZS catalogue](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/PROJEKTI/SDC_WBD/TOOLKIT/General-Information/CZS-katalog-ponudnikov-cebelarskega-turizma.pdf)).

The visit takes the tint band because Beechworth gives its one deep block to "Visit Us" as the emotional high point of the page. Here the tint plays that role, since the dark budget goes to the footer, and a pale green suits photographs of the park, the pond and the Mura, 300 m away through the forest. The current production and seasons sections (`semen-year-wheel`, `semen-seasons`) move to a honey guide page. If one stays on the homepage, it may take a second linden band only where that band does not touch the visit band.

For a shorter page, sections 3 to 5 can collapse into one row of three equal arch-topped images (Zgodba, Obisk, Kakovost), with titles set below each image rather than over a dark overlay. That is the version closest to old Rast, and lighter than its dark-tinted tiles. Rast's map of origins also translates directly into an optional signature band, a map of Šemen's pastures across Slovenia, if the client can supply the locations. Spacing follows one scale throughout: 64, 96 or 128 px above and below each section on desktop, scaling to roughly 40, 56 or 72 px on mobile, with 32–48 px from a heading to its content and content no wider than 1200–1280 px.

### The footer is the one dark close

The footer is linden #2E3A29 with #F1EFE6 text (10.39:1), #BFC3B2 secondary text (6.65:1) and #D6B077 headings (5.89:1). The brand gold also works here (4.71:1), so the crest and fine rules can appear in their true colour. The newsletter sits at the top of the footer block with an underline-style field and no discount offer. Secondary text uses a lower opacity of the light text colour rather than mid-greys, as La Cabra does at 75%, which keeps the footer warm ([lacabra.com](https://lacabra.com)). Quality marks appear as small single-colour marks, the way old Rast and Pump Street showed certifications and awards, and only when currently held (see the last section).

One signature detail is enough: a fine single-line drawing of Šemen's beehouse, or a large "Šemen" wordmark like the one Manukora ends its page with ([manukora.com](https://manukora.com/)). On mobile the link columns fold into expandable lists. The all-light alternative is a wheat #F3EBDA footer with dark text, and Mieli Thun, Savannah Bee, Tiptree and Pump Street all end on light footers ([mielithun.it](https://www.mielithun.it/); [savannahbee.com](https://savannahbee.com/)).

### Build it as section settings, not content edits

Under the project's rules, the theme editor owns `config/settings_data.json`, the template JSON files and the section-group JSON files, so the redesign should be built in a way the client can try in the editor himself. Each `semen-*` section gets a colour-scheme choice (base, tint or dark) linked to CSS variables, padding choices (none, small, medium, large), a "show divider" checkbox and an image-shape choice (rectangle, arch or circle). The 22 hard-coded `#2b2420` values in the 13 code files move into a single variable defined in `snippets/header-css.liquid`. The seven further occurrences in the local template JSON belong to the editor and may be out of date, so those section colours change in the theme editor rather than in code, and the same goes for setting `font_family` to Cormorant and `font_family2` to Jost. Whether Molding 2.0 already has its own colour-scheme settings was not examined, so check that before adding new ones.

## Identical plates, arches and slow fades carry the range

### Product cards: one plate, one angle, eight colours

Across the elegant food shops, the premium signal is consistency plus a quiet background behind each product ([lacabra.com](https://lacabra.com); [flamingoestate.com](https://flamingoestate.com/)). Every jar should be shot the same way: straight on and slightly from above, with the same soft main light plus a light behind the jar so the honey glows, at the same scale and against the same background. Each card is a 4:5 wheat plate (#F3EBDA) with square corners, no border and no shadow, with the jar filling 60–70% of its height and 24–32 px between cards.

Below the plate, the caption starts with a small uppercase label giving season and pasture, stored per product in fields such as `custom.season` and `custom.source`. The name follows in 24 px Cormorant, then "250 g" and the price in Jost with equal-width figures, finished with a small round chip in that honey's colour. Each chip colour should be sampled from a photo of the actual honey; the research team's illustrative range runs from pale acacia #F3E7BF to buckwheat #33231A, but those are not measured honey colours. Homepage cards carry no buttons, hearts or stars, as old Rast had none, and badges appear only as thin outlined tags.

The grid runs from light to dark so it reads like a tasting flight: akacijev, sončnični, lipov, cvetlični, kostanjev, gozdni, hojev, ajdov, the eight products listed in `client-data/build-csv.py`. Manukora tints a plate per grade ([manukora.com](https://manukora.com/)), but it has three grades in muted colours that are not honey-coloured. Eight honey-tinted plates would read as a rainbow, and amber plates swallow amber honey, so one plate colour should let the honey and its chip do the work. Jars shot on white can be blended onto the plate in CSS (`mix-blend-mode: multiply`), but that can dull the shine on the glass, so shooting against a backdrop in the plate colour, or using transparent images, is cleaner.

| Use | Source size | Shape | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desktop hero | 2880 px wide or more | 16:9 to 2.4:1 | Loads first, never lazy |
| Mobile hero | about 1080 × 1350 | 4:5 | Separate crop via `<picture>` |
| Editorial images | about 1200 × 1500 | 4:5, arched top | Story, visit, three-image row |
| Jar photos | 2048 × 2048 or 1600 × 2000 | 1:1 or 4:5, identical for all eight | The grid shows each at a quarter of the page width on desktop |
| Blog cards | about 1200 × 800 | 3:2 | Lazy-loaded |

Each shape gets one role. Rectangles hold the product plates. Arched tops frame the editorial images, echoing both the jar's shoulders and the visible arch under the front of a Slovenian beehouse roof, which the national heritage register describes as guiding the bees to their hives ([Register NKD Rzd-02_00073](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00073.pdf)). That gives a heritage reference without folk ornament. Shopify's reference theme, Dawn, ships an arch mask, so the technique is light and standard ([Dawn assets](https://github.com/Shopify/dawn/tree/main/assets)), and none of the honey sites surveyed use arches, which makes the shape distinctive rather than conventional. Circles are kept for close-up shots of the honey surface from above, the way old Rast showed its coffee, for example in a light-to-dark colour strip on the honey guide page. Hexagons are out: they are a cliché, and the current quality section already uses one.

Product pages need one image that shows scale, such as the jar in a hand, with a spoon or at breakfast, because the Baymard Institute found that **42% of users try to judge a product's size from its images** ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/in-scale-product-images)). The 900 g jars on dark wood suit the story and hero imagery, while the grid uses the plates.

A honey guide page completes the range. Mieli Thun's A–Z honey dictionary and honey-colour guide ([Dizionario](https://www.mielithun.it/dizionario/)) and The Bee Charmer's flavour families ([shopbeecharmer.com](https://shopbeecharmer.com/)) show the format, with colour, aroma, crystallisation, season and food pairings for each honey. Šemen already has this content in its own blog draft (`client-data/blog/01-vrste-medu.html`), and the Slovenski med specification supplies official Slovenian tasting vocabulary, such as chestnut honey's "grenek, trpek, oster" (bitter, astringent, sharp) ([SMGO specification](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/DOKUMENTI/HRANA/SHEME-KAKOVOSTI/SPECIFIKACIJE-EVROPSKA-KOMISIJA/SMGO_potr_sp_7_1_15.pdf)). Slovenian producers rarely publish notes for each honey type, and Čebelarstvo Noč does not, so this is an open gap Šemen can fill.

### Motion and details: short, eased and optional

NN/g puts most interface animation at 100–500 ms: about 100 ms for simple feedback and 200–300 ms for larger changes. It notes that at 500 ms animations "start to feel like a real drag", and it recommends easing that slows toward the end by default ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). Shopify's Dawn theme uses the same scale (100, 200, 300 and 500 ms on the curve `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.3, 1)`) and wraps all of it in a reduced-motion setting ([Dawn base.css](https://github.com/Shopify/dawn/blob/main/assets/base.css)). Šemen's motion follows that scale.

| Element | Movement | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Sections and cards scrolling into view | Fade in while rising 8–16 px, once only, slowing at the end | 300–500 ms; cards in a grid start 60–80 ms apart |
| Links and menu items on hover | Underline draws in from the left | 200–300 ms |
| Buttons on hover | Fill colour changes | 150–200 ms |
| Images on hover | Slow zoom to 1.03–1.05 | Longer; decorative only |

Around those movements, a few rules hold. The hero image is never animated, and only movement and opacity are animated, never layout, as Shopify's performance guidance asks ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/best-practices/performance)). The visitor's reduced-motion setting is always respected ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)). Anything that moves on its own for more than five seconds needs a pause control ([W3C SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)), and parallax, scroll-jacking and bouncy easing are out.

The finishing details are equally quiet. Thin rules are 1 px in the text colour at 10–15% opacity, buttons share the plates' square corners, and on mobile any carousel becomes a swipeable row that shows the edge of the next card and a "2 / 8" counter instead of dots. Signature details should stop at two. The candidates are a gold rule or crest bee as the section opener, a fine single-line drawing of Šemen's own beehouse with its uneven gable roof and front arch, and a narrow story strip in the proportions of a painted beehive panel (about 2.4:1). That strip would be drawn with a flat tinted background and a single outline weight, following the register's description of how the panels are painted ([Register NKD Rzd-02_00065](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00065.pdf)).

## Clichés, folk kitsch and unverified seals are the real traps

### Template tells and honey clichés to leave out

The research team saw the same habits push heritage producers toward generic e-commerce. Famille Mary and Hédène sell fine honey but look like template shops because of promo carousels, discount bands, −15% newsletters, rows of reassurance icons and bold uppercase sans headings ([famillemary.fr](https://www.famillemary.fr/); [hedene.fr](https://hedene.fr/fr/)). Script and novelty fonts make several Slovenian beekeeping sites look homespun rather than premium, such as Dancing Script and Sansita One at Kralov med and Sacramento at Batistuta ([kralov-med.si](https://kralov-med.si/); [cebelarstvo-batistuta.si](https://cebelarstvo-batistuta.si/)), while others still carry the default colours of standard website templates. The Hungarian beekeepers' association (OMME) and a campaign site promoting Slovenia's protected honeys both use the dark-brown-and-gold "honey cliché" the client wants to escape ([omme.hu](https://www.omme.hu/); [okusi-med.eu](https://www.okusi-med.eu/)).

For Šemen that rules out hexagon frames and patterns, cartoon bees and honey drips (at most one subtle honeycomb reference), and two or more dark blocks in the middle of the page. It also rules out auto-playing slideshows, text baked into images, large blocks of saturated colour, the Reference A habits listed earlier, and outline-only text, which the project already bans. Regional brands that win on design do the opposite: one family honey business's rebrand deliberately avoids honey clichés ([Mézes Berek](https://packagingoftheworld.com/2025/10/mezes-berek.html)), and Croatia's Čela stands out against cluttered competitors through "a clean, simple approach" ([Čela](https://packagingoftheworld.com/?p=12184)).

### Quality marks: only what the jar can prove

"Slovenski med z zaščiteno geografsko označbo" was registered by the EU in 2013 and is held by the beekeepers' association ČZS, and only beekeepers or packers with a certificate of conformity may use it ([SMGO specification](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/DOKUMENTI/HRANA/SHEME-KAKOVOSTI/SPECIFIKACIJE-EVROPSKA-KOMISIJA/SMGO_potr_sp_7_1_15.pdf)). Every jar must carry a numbered tamper seal over the lid, issued by ČZS, with the yellow-and-blue EU PGI symbol and a QR code that verifies the beekeeper's certificate, and beekeepers cannot print or buy these seals themselves ([ČZS consumer guide](https://osilnica.si/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Do-vec-informacij-s-QR-kodo-na-Slovenskem-medu_koncna.pdf)). "ŠEMEN Štefan s.p." appears as **no. 372** on the list of certified producers dated 7 December 2020, but that list was **valid only until 3 October 2021** ([MKGP annex](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/PODROCJA/HRANA/SHEME-KAKOVOSTI/CERTIFICIRANI-PROIZVAJALCI-IZBRANA-KAKOVOST/CERTIFICIRANI-PROIZVAJALCI-ZASCITENIH-KMETIJSKIH-PRIDELKOV/SLOVENSKI-MED-priloga.pdf)), so the current status needs confirming with ČZS before the mark appears anywhere.

The current specification (24 September 2025) describes seven honey types: acacia, linden, chestnut, fir, spruce, wildflower and forest. Šemen's sunflower and buckwheat honeys should therefore be treated as outside the scheme unless ČZS confirms otherwise in writing. Because "Slovenski med" is itself the protected name, it should not serve as a headline or product name for jars without the seal. The stakes are higher online, since the EU's current rules for protected food names (Regulation 2024/1143) explicitly strengthen protection for products sold online ([European Commission](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/geographical-indications-and-quality-schemes/geographical-indications-and-quality-schemes-explained_en)). An amendment that would remove the seal template from the specification opened for comment in July 2026, and its outcome is not yet known ([gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/2026-07-08-javni-poziv-za-oddajo-pripomb-na-vlogo-za-spremembo-specifikacije-za-slovenski-med/)). Each mark should therefore appear only where the jar or the business can back it.

| Mark | Condition | Where it may appear |
|---|---|---|
| Slovenski med ZGO with the EU PGI symbol | A valid ČZS certificate and a numbered seal on each jar | Only on product pages for sealed batches, ideally with a photo of the seal and QR code and a link to ČZS's verification. Never as a site-wide badge, or as decoration in the header or footer |
| "Zlati panj" national quality scheme (at most 18% moisture, HMF at most 15 mg/kg) | Listed as a certified scheme in 2025 ([MKGP 2025](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/PODROCJA/HRANA/SHEME-KAKOVOSTI/Slovenski_zasciteni_kmetijski_pridelki_2025.pdf)) | Only if Šemen holds it |
| New Slovenian-flag mark (from 21 March 2026) | A certificate is required, and honey is not explicitly named ([RTV SLO](https://www.rtvslo.si/enostavno/uporabno/oznaka-slovenskih-izdelkov/776259)) | Not until eligibility is confirmed |
| ČZS apitourism certificate (one to three bees) | Rated by ČZS; criteria include presenting the offer attractively ([ČZS catalogue](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/PROJEKTI/SDC_WBD/TOOLKIT/General-Information/CZS-katalog-ponudnikov-cebelarskega-turizma.pdf)) | The visit page, only with a current certificate |
| UNESCO "Beekeeping in Slovenia, a way of life" (2022) | National intangible heritage ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01857)) | Mention in text only; the rules for using the UNESCO emblem were not researched |

### Folk heritage: honour Gorenjska, speak Prekmurje

Painted beehive panels (panjske končnice) are spruce boards about 30 × 12.5 cm; the oldest dated panel is from 1758, and the tradition peaked between 1820 and 1880 ([gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/)). They were painted in earth pigments, and the Selca workshop's palette of "red, Indian red, brown, blue, ochre and green" ([Loški muzej](https://www.loski-muzej.si/en/painted-beehive-panels-from-the-selca-workshop-in-selca/)) is where terracotta can live on, as an accent in illustrations. They are not, however, a Prekmurje tradition. The custom belongs to the Alps and Gorenjska, and the government's heritage article states there were none in lowland Pannonia ([gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/)). Prekmurje kept its bees in straw-and-wicker skeps plastered with clay and set outdoors under the eaves ([Register NKD Rzd-02_00073](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00073.pdf)).

Šemen's painted beehouse should therefore be presented as Slovenian heritage the family honours, photographed where it stands, never as local tradition, and the straw skep is the authentic local motif for line drawings. Printed or acrylic souvenir panels are out, since the heritage authority itself treats them as signs of the craft's decline ([craft sheet](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MGTS/Dokumenti/DNT/Rokodelci/tema/panoge/Poslikavanje-panjskih-koncnic.docx)). So are the satirical panel scenes that mock women, such as the "babji mlin" (women's mill) ([Register NKD Rzd-02_00065](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00065.pdf)).

A few smaller cautions apply as well. The Carniolan bee is shared heritage, since Austrian Carinthia also markets Carnica honey as its own ([kaernten.at](https://www.kaernten.at/kulinarik/typisch-kaerntnerisch/carnica-honig/)), so the copy should say "our Carniolan bee" without claiming exclusivity. The stork is a genuine Prekmurje symbol, but a declining one and prone to baby-themed kitsch, so one small line drawing is the limit. The apitherapy beehouse should be described plainly (40 adapted hives, air rising through openings in two beds, a rest room and a balcony over the park and pond), with no health or cure claims until they have been checked against EU claims rules, which this research did not cover. Finally, the ČZS catalogue lists nine honey varieties while the shop sells eight, and the two should be aligned.

## Conclusion

Most of this redesign is subtraction, plus giving each colour, font and shape one job. The client's sense of heaviness comes from how the dark is spread across the page, and live Rast shows how a light brand drifts heavy one section at a time. So the dark budget should be built into the code, with one dark colour scheme per page applied to the footer by default, rather than left to taste. Keeping gold to ornament makes it read as precious rather than everywhere. And the distinctiveness every honey shop chases is already present in facts no competitor can copy: a 40-hive beehouse for breathing in hive air, about 700 colonies moved around Slovenia, the Mura floodplain and a painted beehouse. Presented with restraint, these do more than any honeycomb or folk pattern.

The steps that will hold the project up are not code. Consistent jar photography decides whether the plate system looks curated or improvised, and a confirmed ZGO status decides whether the strongest trust signal on the page can be used at all. Several things also remain unchecked: no palette has been shown on the store with real photographs, APCA (the newer contrast method proposed for WCAG 3) was not computed, nobody knows whether Shopify's served font files keep small capitals, and the live store's current heading font is unconfirmed. Showing the linden-and-ivory system on the development server with real jar photos before sign-off is the cheapest way to close those gaps.
