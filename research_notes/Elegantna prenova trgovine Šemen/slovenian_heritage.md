# Slovenian beekeeping visual heritage, protected honey marks, and how regional honey/apitourism brands present themselves (for the Čebelarstvo Šemen redesign)

Scope note: researched 2026-10-20. Heritage facts come mainly from primary Slovenian sources: the national intangible-heritage register (Ministry of Culture), MKGP quality-scheme documents, the ČZS Slovenski med specification, UNESCO, and museums. The website survey (Q2) combines a scripted pass over each homepage's HTML and up to 8 linked stylesheets (font-family names and the most frequent non-neutral hex colours, with pure white/black, greys and WordPress/Bootstrap preset colours filtered out) with text fetches of selected pages. The counts show how often a colour appears in the CSS, not how much of the screen it covers. Nothing was judged from screenshots, so the "premium/dated" calls are inferences.

## Q1. What are the defining colours and motifs of panjske končnice, and how could a restrained modern site echo them?

### Takeaway
Končnice are narrow spruce boards about 30 × 12.5 cm. They were painted from the mid-18th century (oldest dated panel 1758, peak 1820–1880) with home-ground earth pigments in linseed oil. A documented workshop palette is red, Indian red, brown, blue, ochre and green. The method was a flat painted ground, a stencilled outline, flat colour fields and brushed contours. The subjects are mostly saints and Bible scenes, plus satirical "topsy-turvy world" scenes. Beekeeping itself is rarely depicted. A restrained site should borrow the *format* (narrow frieze, symmetrical two- or three-part layout), the *technique* (flat ground plus a single-weight contour line) and the *earth-pigment family* as small accents. It should not reproduce full-colour panels.

### Cited Findings
**History and geography**
- Panel painting probably began toward the end of the 18th century. The oldest known panel is dated 1758 and shows Mary with the Child. The classic period was the first three-quarters of the 19th century, especially 1820–1880 — [gov.si, "Zgodbe s panjskih končnic" (6 Dec 2020)](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/)
- The practice started in the Alpine area around Radovljica and Kranj, spread toward the Pannonian plain and across the Karavanke into Carinthia. The same article states there were none in lowland Slovenia, Pannonia, eastern Dolenjska and Bela krajina ("pa jih ni") — [gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/)
- The national register gives the rare dated finds from the late 18th to early 19th century as coming from the Kranj and Kamnik area. It traces later spread to southern Carinthia, NW Styria, central and northern Carniola and part of Goriška. At the end of the 20th and start of the 21st century they are found mainly in Gorenjska. At least 42 named painters formed three stylistic groups — [Register NKD, Rzd-02_00065 "Poslikavanje panjskih končnic"](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00065.pdf)
- End dates differ. ČZS says painting died out after kranjič hives were abandoned in the early 20th century ([ČZS booklet "Slovensko čebelarstvo"](https://czs.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CZS-Knjizica-Slovensko-cebelarstvo-SLO-WEB.pdf)). The Ministry's craft sheet says it faded "until the start of WWII" ([gov.si craft sheet](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MGTS/Dokumenti/DNT/Rokodelci/tema/panoge/Poslikavanje-panjskih-koncnic.docx)). Loški muzej says painting continued in places until the end of WWI ([Loški muzej](https://www.loski-muzej.si/en/painted-beehive-panels-from-the-selca-workshop-in-selca/))
- The panels had a practical use: they helped bees find their hive and beekeepers tell hives (and ownership) apart. They were also thought to protect against curses and misfortune — [gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/); [Rzd-02_00065](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00065.pdf)

**Materials, technique and colour**
- Panels were mostly spruce, less often linden, pine or larch. Painters ground pigments and mixed them with linseed oil, and the images "kljubovale soncu in dežju" (withstood sun and rain). Later panels in industrial paint last much less well — [gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/)
- Process (register): the board is first coated with oil paint "ki predstavlja barvno ozadje slike" (which forms the colour background). The outline is then drawn freehand or transferred with a pricked paper stencil dusted with crushed charcoal. Last come "poslikava posameznih ploskev z barvami in slikanje obrisov s čopičem" (filling individual areas with colour, then painting the outlines with a brush). Boards are 12–15 cm high and of varying length — [Rzd-02_00065](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00065.pdf); [gov.si craft sheet](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MGTS/Dokumenti/DNT/Rokodelci/tema/panoge/Poslikavanje-panjskih-koncnic.docx)
- Documented palette of the Selca workshop (Škofja Loka area, the most prominent folk-painting workshop there): "red, Indian red, brown, blue, ochre and green in various shades". Faces are typified, and roles are shown only through clothing and tasks. The workshop offered 141 subjects (71 religious, 70 secular), and one seller reportedly sold 500 panels a year. Dated Selca panels run from 1864 to 1889 — [Loški muzej](https://www.loski-muzej.si/en/painted-beehive-panels-from-the-selca-workshop-in-selca/)
- A contemporary traditional painter (Anja Bunderla, entered in the register) grinds local rocks into pigment. Her stated reason: "Pigmenti iz domačega okolja so zelo pomembni, da se barvne palete motivov … nevpadljivo vključujejo v prostor" (local pigments matter so that the palettes blend unobtrusively into the surroundings). She paints the board in a lighter shade first and builds up a multi-layer oil image — [Rzd-02_00065](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00065.pdf)
- One secondary travel blog calls the panels "an array of bright colors" that make an apiary look like "an open-air art gallery", with colour helping bees find their own hive. This is a popular source and sits in tension with the earth-pigment accounts above — [livetheworld.com](https://www.livetheworld.com/post/panjska-koncnica-the-art-of-decorating-a-slovenian-beehive-smmh)

**Format and composition**
- The 30 × 12.5 cm format is unusually wide for its height. Painters narrowed the width with drapes or curtains, which give "the feeling of stage view". The low height ruled out tall objects and perspective. Motifs were adapted into "symmetrical twopartite or tripartite" compositions — [Giesemann, Traditiones (ZRC SAZU), abstract and English summary](https://ojs.zrc-sazu.si/traditiones/article/view/1094); [full text PDF](https://ojs.zrc-sazu.si/traditiones/article/download/1094/873/2981)
- Side ornament appears too: Giesemann describes a satirical scene flanked by "umetelnimi amforami in … stilizacijami vrtnic" (elaborate amphorae and stylised roses) — [Giesemann full text](https://ojs.zrc-sazu.si/traditiones/article/download/1094/873/2981)

**Motifs**
- Religious: Mary (the most popular, as universal protector), saints and their legends, scenes from the Old and New Testament, St Florian, Job, and the duel of Pegam and Lambergar. Secular: trades and work, animals, exotic scenes, unusual events, soldiers, hunting, relations between the sexes, animals in human roles, and "pogosto satirično smešenje ženskih lastnosti" (frequent satirical mockery of women's traits) — [Rzd-02_00065](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00065.pdf)
- Punishment is the most common motif. There are no portraits, little landscape, no erotic scenes and few depictions of beekeeping. Popular secular scenes include the "babji mlin" (women's mill), Shrovetide customs and the "žaganje babe" (sawing the old woman) — [gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/)
- Secular motifs mirror the social order of their time: "misogynist images, professional hierarchy, and satirization of everyday life, often presented through animals (the so-called 'topsy-turvy world')" — [Giesemann](https://ojs.zrc-sazu.si/traditiones/article/view/1094)

**Status today (souvenir and kitsch risk)**
- Only a few individuals still paint panels the traditional way. Others use acrylics or print, and such panels are sold in beekeeping-supply shops. About ten makers produce them serially in acrylic or print. The craft is described as endangered and as changing through "uporabe industrijskih barv, neuporabe šablon za slikanje in celo uporabe tiska" (industrial paints, no stencils, even printing). Panels are "del spominkarskega repertorija" (part of the souvenir repertoire) and are often given as protocol gifts — [gov.si craft sheet](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MGTS/Dokumenti/DNT/Rokodelci/tema/panoge/Poslikavanje-panjskih-koncnic.docx)

**Collections and image references**
- The Čebelarski muzej Radovljica has operated since 1959. Its "obsežna zbirka poslikanih panjskih končnic" is one of its main treasures, and the permanent exhibition "Živeti skupaj" reopened in 2021 — [SID flyer (2022)](https://www.sid.si/sites/www.sid.si/files/documents/letak_elektronsko_3.pdf)
- The Volkskundemuseum Wien has an online album of 238 digitised beehive front panels ("Bienenstockstirnbretter"), which can serve as a visual reference (seen only in the search listing) — [Volkskundemuseum Wien collection](https://sammlung.volkskundemuseum.at/topic/view/303190)

### Inferences
- **Format as a layout device.** The wide, narrow panel (about 2.4:1) maps naturally onto a thin horizontal frieze: a section divider, a "story strip" or a hero band, with symmetrical two- or three-part content (e.g., jar | scene | jar) and an optional curtain-like framing line. This echoes končnice without showing a painted panel.
- **Technique as an illustration style.** A flat tinted ground, flat fills and one contour weight is exactly the register's described method. Thin single-weight line drawings of the beehouse, the AŽ hive, the Mura and the Carniolan bee on a pale ground would read as "končnica-derived" while staying modern.
- **Palette as accents only.** The documented pigment family (red, Indian red, brown, blue, ochre, green), muted as the register's painter advocates, could supply small accents: variety chips, label bands, link and hover colours, one illustration fill per section. A light base stays in line with the client's dislike of heavy dark-brown sections. (Building the actual palette is out of scope here; other researchers cover it.)
- **Ornament sparingly.** The stylised roses and amphorae on panel sides suggest a single small corner or border ornament at most (e.g., on a heritage or gift page), not a repeating folk pattern.
- **Prefer motif categories over literal scenes.** Beekeeping scenes are rare on authentic panels, so a bee-themed "končnica" graphic is largely a modern invention. Neutral authentic categories (animals, Mary/saints only if culturally appropriate, rural work) are safer than satirical scenes.

### Gaps
- I found no quantified study of colour frequency (e.g., which background colours dominate). The Selca list is one workshop.
- Collection size at Radovljica is unresolved. Secondary sources found via search conflict ("more than 800" vs "approximately 250"), and the museum site (mro.si) returned bot verification to all fetches. One search snippet said more than 600 distinct motifs are known across the SEM and Radovljica collections, but I could not pin that to a specific document.
- I found no verified case study of a premium brand reinterpreting končnice in a restrained, modern way. The Radovljica museum's visual identity could not be inspected.

## Q2. Which Slovenian or Central European honey/apitourism sites look premium and why; which look dated?

### Takeaway
The sites that read as premium (Mieli Thun, Honigmayr, Čebelarstvo Noč, Jere Meadery, and Mr. Bee in part) share three traits: a restrained warm palette (cream or off-white plus one deep accent and one honey or gold tone), distinctive or custom typography, and a clear product system (monofloral taxonomy, tasting vocabulary, gift lines, seasonal selection). Dated sites show template-default colours (Bootstrap, Magento, Divi), script or novelty display fonts, saturated yellow/red, and stale news feeds. Several institutional honey sites (OMME, Okusi med) lean on the dark-brown plus gold "honey cliché" the client wants to avoid.

### Inferences
- **Premium-leaning.** Mieli Thun is the clearest regional benchmark: a warm cream/orange/charcoal system, a named tasting method, seasonal curation, minimal packaging, one commissioned illustrator, and a manifesto. Honigmayr shows how a custom typeface plus cream and near-black reads modern and light even for a mass brand. Čebelarstvo Noč is the closest Slovenian peer in story (family, AŽ, Carniolan, gifts) and uses deep green instead of brown with cream and gold, a useful precedent for a lighter heritage look. Jere and Mr. Bee show that a serif or custom type with one metallic accent signals "premium" in Slovenia.
- **Dated or template-looking.** Stadtimker (no webfonts, red/yellow), IMEGO (Jimdo, Cambria), Čebelji gradič and BEe in foREST (colours matching Bootstrap 3 defaults), the Moravske Toplice and ZTKŠ Beltinci tourism sites (colours matching Bootstrap 4 defaults; Beltinci adds a novelty "rustic" display font), Dr Honey ("Prémium" claim undercut by the Divi default blue and a techno font), and Medex (Magento defaults and a supplements-first message). Script fonts (Dancing Script, Sacramento) and novelty display faces (Sansita One, Berkshire Swash) at Kralov med, Batistuta and Lectar push those sites toward "folksy" rather than premium.
- **Heavy-brown cliché.** OMME (#482727 dominant) and the Okusi med campaign site for Slovenia's protected honeys (#471d11 plus gold) show the dark-brown and honey-gold look the client dislikes. Šemen can stand apart simply by not doing it.
- **Content gap to exploit.** Few Slovenian producer sites give per-variety tasting notes, seasons or harvest geography (Noč does not). The SMGO specification's official sensory descriptors (see Q3) plus Šemen's migratory beekeeping could give Šemen a Mieli Thun-style variety system rooted in Slovenia.
- **Visits as product.** Slovenian providers increasingly sell the experience itself (aerosol sessions, workshops) as a bookable product. Šemen's beehouse (see Q4) fits that pattern.

### Gaps
- No screenshots were taken, so imagery quality (photography style, illustration use) was not assessed directly. Colour and font readings are heuristic.
- Croatian and Hungarian producer *websites* with premium design were hard to find. Hungarian and Croatian evidence is mostly packaging case studies. No Croatian producer homepage was surveyed, and the Croatian association site did not respond.

## Q3. What are the rules for using the Slovenski med ZGO mark (it may only be used for certified honey)?

### Takeaway
"Slovenski med" (PGI, registered 2013, holder ČZS) may be used only by certified beekeepers or fillers. Each jar must carry a numbered ČZS-issued tamper seal ("zaščitna prelepka") with the EU PGI symbol and a QR code that verifies the beekeeper's certificate. Beekeepers cannot print these seals themselves. Šemen Štefan s.p. appears as no. 372 on the certified-producer annex dated 7 Dec 2020, but that annex was valid only until 3 Oct 2021, so current status must be confirmed. The specification characterises seven honey types, and sunflower and buckwheat are not among them.

### Inferences
- **Web use.** The EU PGI symbol and "Slovenski med z zaščiteno geografsko označbo" should appear only on product pages for lots actually sold under a ČZS seal, never as a site-wide decorative badge. Showing the seal or QR in product photos and linking to ČZS's verification would turn compliance into a premium trust signal. That fits the Commission's stated focus on GI misuse online.
- **Sunflower and buckwheat.** Neither appears among the types characterised in the specification (or in the 2021 list), so they likely cannot be sold as "Slovenski med ZGO" unless ČZS confirms otherwise. The site would need two tiers: ZGO-sealed varieties (acacia, linden, chestnut, forest, fir, wildflower) and unsealed "Slovenian-origin" varieties.
- **Wording.** "Slovenski med" is itself the registered name, so the shop should avoid using it as a product name or headline for uncertified honey. Legal wording should be confirmed with ČZS or MKGP.
- **Tasting notes.** The specification's sensory profiles give authoritative Slovenian vocabulary for variety notes on the site.

## Q4. Which local or heritage references would give this shop distinctive character without kitsch, and which pitfalls to avoid?

### Takeaway
The most distinctive references that can be verified for Šemen are:
1. Its own certified-style apitherapy beehouse and 700-colony AŽ operation.
2. The Slovenian čebelnjak silhouette (cube body, asymmetric roof, front arch) and the grid of coloured AŽ hive fronts.
3. The Carniolan bee, the UNESCO-inscribed "way of life", and World Bee Day.
4. Prekmurje's own landscape and heritage: the Mura floodplain inside the five-country UNESCO biosphere reserve, floating mills and ferries, white storks, pumpkin-seed oil, gibanica, and the region's straw-skep tradition.

Pitfalls: presenting painted panels as a Prekmurje tradition (they historically did not occur there), printed folk-panel souvenirs and cartoon bees, misogynist satirical panel scenes, decorative misuse of the PGI or UNESCO marks, and the dark-brown and gold honey cliché.

### Cited Findings
**Šemen's own heritage asset (from the ČZS apitourism catalogue)**
- "Apiturizem Šemen Štefan, Lipa 145 A, 9231 Beltinci". The beehouse for inhaling aromas of wax, honey and propolis has "40 prirejenih panjev za uživanje aerosola z dvema ležiščema in dvema udobnima sedežema" (40 adapted hives, two beds, two comfortable seats). Aerosol rises through round openings in the specially adapted beds, so no inhaler masks are needed, and intensity is set by opening or closing the openings. Fresh pollen can be taken straight from the hive. There is an upper-floor rest room and a balcony overlooking a park and pond. The entry states "Čebelarimo s 700 družinami. Čebele prevažamo na različne paše po Sloveniji." — [ČZS catalogue of apitourism providers](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/PROJEKTI/SDC_WBD/TOOLKIT/General-Information/CZS-katalog-ponudnikov-cebelarskega-turizma.pdf)
- Offer listed: visits to several locations with AŽ and DB hives; "9 sort medu – posebnost ajdov med" (9 honey varieties, buckwheat a speciality), plus propolis, pollen and royal jelly; "Aktiven prevoz čebel na vozu, kot so vozili na pašo nekoč" (moving bees by cart, as they used to); lodging for up to 6 with bee aerosol throughout the apartment; picnic by the pond; fishing; groups up to 50 — [ČZS catalogue](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/PROJEKTI/SDC_WBD/TOOLKIT/General-Information/CZS-katalog-ponudnikov-cebelarskega-turizma.pdf)
- Location: Melinci, 12 km from Murska Sobota; the Mura is 300 m away through forest paths and oxbows ("mrtvice"), an otter and beaver area; ferry rides on the Mura at Gornja Bistrica (2 km); spas at Banovci (4 km), Moravske Toplice (15 km) and Lendava (15 km) — [ČZS catalogue](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/PROJEKTI/SDC_WBD/TOOLKIT/General-Information/CZS-katalog-ponudnikov-cebelarskega-turizma.pdf). (The catalogue is undated but cites 39 certified providers "in 2017". It says 9 varieties, while the brief says 8.)

**Čebelnjak (beehouse) architecture**
- From the register: beehouses are farm buildings for hive colonies and the beekeeper's work, a characteristic of the Slovenian cultural landscape, with nearly 14,000 registered. Hives sit in the open front, "katerih čelnice so pogosto poslikane ali pobarvane" (whose fronts are often painted or coloured), and are worked from the back. "Arhitekturne posebnosti slovenskega čebelnjaka so lesena ali kamnita konstrukcija … v obliki kocke ali kvadra, običajno krita z nesimetrično dvokapno streho. Zadnji del strehe je položnejši in daljši, sprednji (napušč) pa krajši, višji in strmejši … opazni lok pod njim pa usmerja čebele k panjem." The best orientation is SE. Some beehouses have a figural hive acting as "čuvaj čebel" (bee guard): a soldier, bear or dog — [Register NKD, Rzd-02_00073 "Izdelovanje panjev in čebelnjakov"](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00073.pdf)
- ČZS: the ideal beehouse is a wooden farm building that fits most Slovenian settlement and landscape forms (stone partly replaces wood in the Karst-Littoral). It often includes a "ležišče" (bed) for the beekeeper's rest, and its microclimate now makes it widely used for apitherapy — [ČZS booklet](https://czs.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CZS-Knjizica-Slovensko-cebelarstvo-SLO-WEB.pdf)
- 2020 figures: 11,293 beekeepers, 15,420 apiaries and more than 210,000 colonies (via search summary) — [gov.si, Slovenija in čebelarstvo](https://www.gov.si/zbirke/projekti-in-programi/svetovni-dan-cebel/slovenija-in-cebelarstvo)

**Prekmurje's own hive tradition (important for authenticity)**
- "V Beli krajini, ponekod na Štajerskem in v Prekmurju so uporabljali čebelje koše z nepremičnim satjem, pletene iz slame in šibja, tudi premazane z mešanico ilovice in kravjaka ter pokrite s slamnato streho. Postavljali so jih na prosto, pod napušče, redkeje v čebelnjake." (Straw and wicker skeps, plastered with clay and cow dung, thatched, kept outdoors under eaves) — [Rzd-02_00073](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00073.pdf). This matches the gov.si statement that painted panels were absent in Pannonia — [gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/)

**AŽ hive**
- "Po letu 1910 se je uveljavil Alberti-Žnideršičev panj ('AŽ-panj' ali 'žnideršič') velikosti 41 x 26 cm s premičnim satjem, ki ga je po vzoru nemškega učitelja Albertija razvil Anton Žnideršič." More than 90% of Slovenian beekeepers use it, and it suits both stacking in beehouses and migratory transport. The basic type has 10 frames ("desetsatar"). It replaced the kranjič, a hive of about 30 × 16 × 75 cm whose fronts were painted from the 18th century — [Rzd-02_00073](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00073.pdf)
- Anton Žnideršič (1874–1947), a merchant, landowner, industrialist and large-scale beekeeper from Ilirska Bistrica, designed it about a hundred years ago. It spread across Slovenia and to Croatia and is considered the best hive for migratory beekeeping, suitable even for older beekeepers, women and people with disabilities — [ČZS booklet](https://czs.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CZS-Knjizica-Slovensko-cebelarstvo-SLO-WEB.pdf)

**Carniolan bee (kranjska čebela / kranjska sivka)**
- Apis mellifera carnica (Pollmann 1879) is a protected subspecies in Slovenia and the world's second most widespread honeybee subspecies — [ČZS booklet](https://czs.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CZS-Knjizica-Slovensko-cebelarstvo-SLO-WEB.pdf). UNESCO: "The only subspecies kept in Slovenia is the Carniolan bee" — [UNESCO ICH](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01857)
- The Carniolan bee spread worldwide from 1868 (Baron Emil Rothschütz's bee trade; shippers Mihael Ambrožič of Mojstrana and Jan Strgar of Bohinj) — [SMGO specification, history section](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/DOKUMENTI/HRANA/SHEME-KAKOVOSTI/SPECIFIKACIJE-EVROPSKA-KOMISIJA/SMGO_potr_sp_7_1_15.pdf)
- Not exclusive to Slovenia: Carinthia (Austria) markets "Carnica-Honig" as a typical regional speciality, with the Carnica described as native to the Rosental for centuries — [kaernten.at](https://www.kaernten.at/kulinarik/typisch-kaerntnerisch/carnica-honig/). Austrian breeders such as IMEGO also promote Carnica — [imego.at](https://www.imego.at/)

**UNESCO and World Bee Day**
- "Beekeeping in Slovenia, a way of life" was inscribed on the Representative List in 2022 at 17.COM in Rabat (decision 17.COM 7.B.27). It covers about 200,000 colonies, wooden apiaries near homes, "characteristic painted panels and religious and secular motifs on the hives", and traditional apiary architecture — [UNESCO ICH](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01857); [decision](https://ich.unesco.org/en/decisions/17.COM/7.B.27); [gov.si (1 Dec 2022)](https://gov.si/en/news/2022-12-01-beekeeping-in-slovenia-on-the-unesco-representative-list-of-intangible-cultural-heritage)
- World Bee Day is 20 May, the birthday of Anton Janša (1734–1773). ČZS proposed it to the government in 2014 under the slogan "SVETOVNI DAN ČEBEL ZDRUŽUJE SLOVENCE IN POVEZUJE SVET" — [ČZS booklet](https://czs.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CZS-Knjizica-Slovensko-cebelarstvo-SLO-WEB.pdf). The UN General Assembly proclaimed it on 20 Dec 2017 by consensus with 115 co-sponsors (via search summary) — [gov.si, World Bee Day](https://www.gov.si/en/registries/projects/world-bee-day/about/); [Slovenia Times](https://sloveniatimes.com/12750/un-declares-20-may-world-bee-day-on-slovenias-initiative)
- Janša taught at the world's first beekeeping school in Vienna (1771–1773), wrote "Vollständige Lehre von der Bienenzucht" (1773), and introduced migratory beekeeping in that part of Europe — [SMGO specification](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MKGP/DOKUMENTI/HRANA/SHEME-KAKOVOSTI/SPECIFIKACIJE-EVROPSKA-KOMISIJA/SMGO_potr_sp_7_1_15.pdf)

**Apitherapy heritage (relevant to Šemen's beehouse)**
- Apitherapy is entered in the national register. Newer forms include adapted beehouses where users relax, watch and listen to bees, or "vdihavajo zrak iz čebeljih panjev (aerosol)", with reported benefits to wellbeing and the airways. Dr Filip Terc (1844–1917) of Maribor is considered the founder of modern apitherapy, and his birthday, 30 March, was set as World Apitherapy Day in 2006 (Passau). ČZS runs a Komisija za apiterapijo — [Register NKD, Rzd-02_00079 "Apiterapija"](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00079.pdf)

**Prekmurje and Mura imagery**
- The Mura–Drava–Danube Transboundary Biosphere Reserve was declared by UNESCO on 15 Sep 2021. It is the world's first five-country reserve (AT, SI, HR, HU, RS), about 1 million ha and 700 km of rivers, called the "Amazon of Europe". Its habitats include floodplain forests, gravel and sand bars, river islands and oxbows, with otter, beaver and black stork; Slovenia's component dates from 2018 — [IUCN](https://www.iucn.org/news/protected-areas/202201/unesco-declares-worlds-first-5-country-biosphere-reserve-along-mura-drava-danube); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/wildlife-biodiversity/unesco-declares-world-s-first-5-country-biosphere-reserve-in-amazon-of-europe--79056)
- Ižakovci (Beltinci municipality) has the "Otok ljubezni" (Island of Love). The floating mill sank into the Mura in 2022, and a new mill and ferry opened in October 2024 through the ReKult project; the tourism institute hoped for 50,000 visitors — [Svet24](https://svet24.si/clanek/prekmurje-mlin-na-muri-otok-ljubezni-1863590); [Wikipedia: Ižakovci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C5%BEakovci)
- White stork: called a symbol of Prekmurje — [Svet24](https://svet24.si/zivali/foto-778004); [RTV SLO](https://www.rtvslo.si/moja-generacija/novice/storklje-na-poti-v-toplejse-kraje/432850). In 2020, 259 pairs nested in Slovenia, mostly in the NE, about 80% on electricity poles and about 15% on chimneys. The dialect name is "bejli štrk" — [Žurnal24](https://www.zurnal24.si/gorenjska/na-trati-ze-cakajo-storklje-in-mladicke-377861). Numbers are rising elsewhere but falling in Prekmurje — [N1](https://n1info.si/novice/slovenija/letos-v-sloveniji-rekordno-stevilo-storkelj-izginjajo-le-v-prekmurju/). **Sourced kitsch signals and pitfalls**
- The heritage authority itself treats serially printed or acrylic panels and the missing stencil technique as signs of the craft's decline, and notes panels' role as souvenirs — [gov.si craft sheet](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MGTS/Dokumenti/DNT/Rokodelci/tema/panoge/Poslikavanje-panjskih-koncnic.docx)
- Traditional panel motifs often include misogynist satire ("babji mlin", "žaganje babe", a wife riding on her husband's cross) — [Rzd-02_00065](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MK/DEDISCINA/NESNOVNA/RNSD_SI/Rzd-02_00065.pdf); [gov.si](https://www.gov.si/novice/zgodbe-s-panjskih-koncnic/); [Giesemann](https://ojs.zrc-sazu.si/traditiones/article/download/1094/873/2981)
- Premium honey rebrands in the region explicitly avoid "honey clichés" (Mézes Berek) and stand out through a clean, simple approach against cluttered competitors (Čela) — [Mézes Berek](https://packagingoftheworld.com/2025/10/mezes-berek.html); [Čela](https://packagingoftheworld.com/?p=12184)

### Inferences
- **Lead with Šemen's own story, then frame the national heritage.** The 40-hive aerosol beehouse, 700 colonies moved around Slovenia, AŽ hives and the Mura setting are unique facts that no competitor can copy. The UNESCO "way of life", the Carniolan bee and World Bee Day work as context, not as the headline.
- **Beehouse as the brand icon.** The asymmetric roof profile with its front arch, drawn as a fine line, is a distinctive, non-kitsch emblem that also fits the heraldic crest. The front grid of coloured AŽ hive fronts is a natural colour-coding system for the honey varieties (one muted earth-pigment chip per variety) and a recurring layout grid.
- **Local, not borrowed.** Painted panels are a Gorenjska and Alpine tradition, so present them as Slovenian heritage that Šemen honours (e.g., one commissioned, hand-painted panel on the beehouse photographed in situ), never as Prekmurje tradition. The Prekmurje straw skep is a little-used, authentic local motif for line illustration.
- **Mura and plains for atmosphere.** Light, airy photography of the floodplain forest, oxbows, the ferry and floating mill, and open plains suits the "lighter, more elegant" brief better than dark interiors. Mentioning the UNESCO biosphere reserve in text (not with its logo) adds credibility.
- **Use the stork carefully.** It is a genuine regional symbol but declining locally and widely used in baby-themed kitsch. A single small line drawing (e.g., in the footer or on the "about the region" page) is the safe dose. Pumpkin-oil green and gibanica's four-layer stripe are possible secondary accents for regional or gift content, but risk tipping into tourist-brochure cuteness if overused.
- **Calendar hooks.** 20 May (World Bee Day), 30 March (World Apitherapy Day, Terc), and the 1 Dec 2022 UNESCO anniversary give natural content dates.
- **Avoid:** full-colour panel reproductions as backgrounds or patterns; cartoon yellow-black bees; hexagon and honey-drip overload; script or "rustic" display fonts; saturated yellow/red; dark-brown blocks; satirical or misogynist panel scenes; the PGI symbol or UNESCO emblem used decoratively; health or cure claims for honey or apitherapy (regulatory risk, see Gaps).

### Gaps
- UNESCO emblem use: I did not research the rules for commercial use of the UNESCO/ICH emblem. Treat it as text-only until checked.
- Health-claim limits for honey and apitherapy marketing (EU nutrition and health claims rules) were not researched, yet the register describes apitherapy in "preventive and curative" terms.
- I did not verify whether Šemen holds a current ČZS apitourism certificate (1–3 "čebelice"), or confirm the "Top Bee" tier mentioned in one secondary source.
- The Carniolan bee's visual traits (the greyish "sivka") were not sourced beyond the name. Any bee illustration's colouring should be checked with ČZS.
- Beltinci-specific heritage (castle, Beltinška banda, local crafts) was not verified in this round.
